Risk

Risk is a combination of probability and impact. Therefore, to lower risk, one has to either lower the probability of an event, or to lower it’s impact.

Systemic vs. ideosyncratic risk

Liquidity cascades, short and gamma risks, leverage.

Counterparty risk, concentration risk, liquidity risk, solvency risk, model risk, implementation risk, rehypothecation risk.

Types of risks related to implementation:

  • Bugs..
  • unreliable latency
  • faulty risk engine

architectural or design faults tend to show up precisely when the system is stress tested by extreme events

Priority of concerns

Optimize in this order:

  1. Correctness
  2. Determinism
  3. Risk isolation
  4. Observability
  5. Latency

Not the other way around. Bad state at 50us is worse than correct state at 500us.

Kill switches

Have all of these:

  • Global kill switch
  • Per-venue kill switch
  • Per-strategy kill switch
  • Latency spike kill switch
  • PnL drawdown auto-disable

Your kill switch must be faster than your strategy. If your strategy can do damage in 50ms, your kill switch must act in 10ms.

Hard position limits are enforced at the order router level, not the strategy level. The strategy is compromised when you need the kill switch.

Test them. A kill switch that has never fired in production is a kill switch that might not work.

Fat finger filters

Non-negotiable. Enforce independently:

  • Max order size
  • Max notional per second
  • Max orders per second

Exchanges have fat finger protections too but don’t rely on them.

Circuit breakers

Trigger on:

  • PnL drawdown (not just position — a flat position with massive realized losses is still a crisis)
  • Spread widening velocity
  • Order book depth decay
  • Funding spike
  • Liquidation bursts

Risk checks: hot path vs. cold path

Hot path: Single atomic flag read. if halt_flag → stop. One instruction.

Cold path: Full risk evaluation — position limits, exposure, delta, drawdown. Runs independently, sets the flag when limits are breached.

Never let risk checks block signal generation. Risk writes flags, hot path reads flags.

“Your risk engine should be an independent task/process. Last gate before order send. Deterministic. Not async spaghetti.”

Defense in depth

Pre-trade:    sim checks, rate limits, fat finger filters
In-trade:     circuit breakers, inventory caps
Post-trade:   reconciliation, auto-hedge/flatten
Meta:         kill-all button (API key revoke script ready)

One missed layer = game over.

The strategy should never be able to

  • Overexpose capital
  • Double position accidentally
  • Trade when out of sync
  • Trade during reconciliation mismatch
  • Trade when price feed stale
