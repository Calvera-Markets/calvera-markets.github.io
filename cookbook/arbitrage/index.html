Arbitrage

Arb is inventory management

You don’t predict. You:

  • Move inventory
  • Neutralize exposure
  • Minimize imbalance duration
  • Minimize capital lockup time

PnL is a side effect of capital velocity, inventory discipline, and fee optimization.

Leg risk

The killer nobody talks about enough. You filled leg 1, leg 2 is moving against you — your “arb” is now a directional position.

For latency arb (speed is the edge): Send both legs simultaneously, accept leg risk, price it into entry threshold. Waiting for leg 1 ack costs the round trip — the arb might be gone.

For spread arb (spread is the edge): Leg 2 triggered by OMS when leg 1 fill confirms. Zero leg risk but slower. This is an execution algo concern, not a hot path concern.

Which is correct depends on your edge. For funding rate arb (8-hour holding period), the spread is the edge → sequential leg execution is correct.

Ghost arbs

Common trap:

  1. See spread
  2. Fire both sides
  3. One leg fills
  4. Other leg moves
  5. Now you’re directional

Evaluate before entering:

  • Fill probability
  • Book depth on both sides
  • Adverse selection probability
  • Spread half-life

If spread decays in 20ms and your round trip is 30ms — it’s not an arb.

Fees are alpha

Most crypto arbs die because:

Gross spread > 0
Net spread after fees < 0

Model fees down to the basis point:

  • Maker rebates
  • VIP tier rates
  • Funding payments
  • Borrow costs
  • Withdrawal fees

The difference between paying taker fees and earning maker rebates is often the difference between profitable and not.

Funding rate arb specifics

The carry looks free until:

  • Basis moves and your collateral gets margin called on the perp side
  • Your spot leg becomes illiquid

Model the worst-case basis swing, not the average. Entry threshold should price in the max adverse basis move over your holding period.

Capital efficiency

Arb isn’t about % return. It’s about:

PnL / Capital locked / Time

Optimize for:

  • Fast settlement cycles
  • Minimal inventory skew
  • Collateral reuse
  • Cross-margin efficiency

Adverse selection

In latency arb or market making, the fills you get are often the toxic ones others dodged. Real alpha hides in the ones you miss.

Filter ruthlessly: order flow imbalance, quote stuffing, depth checks before aggressing. Backtest isn’t enough — forward-test with tiny size, watch real adverse PnL.

Market regimes

Arb bot works in tight spreads and calm volatility. Dies in:

  • Sudden volatility expansion
  • Liquidity evaporation
  • Exchange outages

Detect regime shifts:

  • Spread widening velocity
  • Order book depth decay
  • Funding spike
  • Liquidation bursts

And switch modes.

Speed without queue priority is cope

In HFT, speed only matters if:

  • You win queue position
  • You maintain queue priority
  • You understand cancel/replace penalties

Otherwise you’re just fast and irrelevant.

The market is adversarial

If you see something obvious:

  • It’s priced in
  • Or it’s toxic
  • Or you’re the exit liquidity

Assume you’re being picked off until proven otherwise.
